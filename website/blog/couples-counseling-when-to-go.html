Couples Counseling: 7 Signs It Might Be Time to Go

Illinois Valley Counseling Services · 6 min read

Somewhere around the kitchen table, many couples have this conversation: "Do you think we should see someone?" - and then they don't, for years. The average couple waits six years between noticing a serious relationship problem and actually seeking help.

That wait usually makes things harder, not better. Here are seven signs that couples counseling could help now, rather than later.

1. You're Having the Same Fight Over and Over

Money, chores, in-laws, texting habits - it doesn't matter what the subject is. When you keep circling the same conflict with the same outcome, it's a sign the pattern - not the topic - is the problem. A therapist can help you see the pattern and change it.

2. Communication Has Broken Down

Maybe you're walking on eggshells. Maybe conversations turn into arguments within 30 seconds. Maybe you've stopped talking about anything real at all. When communication becomes a minefield, you need a safe space to relearn it.

3. You're Avoiding Each Other

Different schedules, separate hobbies, staying late at work, scrolling phones at dinner. A little space is healthy; a lot of strategically-arranged space is usually avoidance. If you feel more like roommates than partners, that's worth taking seriously.

4. A Major Life Event Has Shaken Things Up

A baby, a move, a job loss, a death in the family, an affair, a health crisis. Big events change relationships - sometimes in ways neither of you expected. Counseling can help you navigate the new normal together instead of drifting apart in it.

5. Resentment Is Building

"I'm always the one who..." - if you fill in that sentence with frustration, resentment is already taking root. Resentment is like a quiet leak: it does damage long before anyone notices the water. It's much easier to fix early.

6. One (or Both) of You Has Mentally Checked Out

You've stopped planning trips. You don't fight anymore - you just don't care. Emotional withdrawal is often the last stage before a breakup, but it doesn't have to be the end of the story. People reconnect all the time, with help.

7. You Still Love Each Other (That's the Key)

Here's the paradox: the couples who most often come to us are the ones who still love each other but don't know how to show it anymore. If there's love underneath the frustration, counseling has something real to work with. You don't need to be on the brink to deserve help.

What Couples Counseling Is (and Isn't)

Counseling isn't about taking sides, assigning blame, or "fixing" one person. It's about giving both of you new tools - communication skills, conflict patterns, repair conversations - and a neutral space to use them. Our therapists work with married couples, partners, and couples at every stage.

Why Sooner Is Better

Patterns get stronger the longer they repeat. The earlier you get help, the less groundwork there is to undo - and the more of your energy goes toward building the relationship you actually want.
