How Do I Know If I Need Therapy? 10 Signs It's Time to Talk to Someone

Illinois Valley Counseling Services · 5 min read

It's a question almost everyone asks themselves at some point: "Is this something I should talk to someone about, or am I overreacting?"

Here's the honest answer: you don't need to be in crisis to benefit from therapy, and you don't need to "prove" you deserve help. If something in your life feels heavy, that's enough of a reason to reach out.

That said, these ten signs are the ones we hear most often from new clients at Illinois Valley Counseling Services - and they're a good starting point for self-reflection.

1. You Feel Stuck or "Off" and Can't Explain Why

You're getting through your days, but something feels wrong. You've lost interest in things you used to enjoy, or you feel flat, numb, or disconnected. You don't have to be able to name the problem to deserve help with it.

2. Anxiety Is Running Your Life

Constant worry, racing thoughts, trouble sleeping, a knot in your stomach that never fully goes away. If anxiety is making decisions hard or keeping you from doing things you want to do, that's a sign it's time to talk to someone.

3. Sadness Has Lingered for Weeks

Everyone has bad days. But when low mood, tearfulness, or emptiness sticks around for two weeks or more - and especially when it's affecting your sleep, appetite, or energy - it's worth taking seriously.

4. You're Using Alcohol, Food, or Screens to Cope

There's a difference between unwinding and coping. If you notice you need more of something - another drink, another hour of scrolling - just to feel okay, it might be time to look at what's underneath.

5. Your Relationships Are Straining

You're snapping at your partner, withdrawing from friends, or dreading family events. Relationship strain is often one of the first visible symptoms of something deeper going on.

6. You've Been Through Something Hard

Trauma, grief, a major loss, a divorce, a health scare, losing a job. You don't have to "get over it" on your own. These experiences change people, and healing often takes support.

7. You Keep Having the Same Fight or the Same Pattern

Whether it's with your partner, your kids, or yourself - if you keep hitting the same wall no matter what you try, a fresh perspective from a trained therapist can help break the cycle.

8. Sleep or Appetite Have Changed

Can't fall asleep, waking up at 3 AM, sleeping 12 hours, or losing your appetite? Your body often signals emotional distress before your mind does.

9. Someone You Trust Has Suggested It

If a partner, friend, parent, or doctor has gently said "maybe talk to someone" - listen. People who care about you can often see what you can't.

10. You Just Have a Gut Feeling

You don't need a checklist to justify reaching out. If you've read this far and something in you said "that's me," that's enough. Trust it.

What to Do Next

The first step is the hardest one, and it's also the simplest: pick up the phone or request a call back. Our office will call you to find a time that works, verify your insurance, and match you with a therapist who fits your needs. It's confidential, it's judgment-free, and there's no obligation.

Change is possible - and you don't have to figure it all out before you take the first step.
